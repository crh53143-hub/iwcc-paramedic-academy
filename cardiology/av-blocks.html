Understanding AV Blocks

AV blocks occur when conduction between the atria and ventricles is delayed or interrupted. The severity ranges from mild conduction delay to complete electrical separation of the atria and ventricles.

Exam Tip: Think of AV blocks as a spectrum:
  • First Degree = delayed
  • Second Degree Type I = progressively delayed then dropped
  • Second Degree Type II = suddenly dropped
  • Third Degree = completely disconnected

First-Degree AV Block

Characteristic Finding
PR Interval > 0.20 seconds
P Waves Every P followed by QRS
Rhythm Usually regular
Dropped Beats None

How to Recognize It

The PR interval is prolonged but every atrial impulse reaches the ventricles.

"If the PR is long and all are gone, it's First Degree."

Second-Degree AV Block Type I (Mobitz I / Wenckebach)

Characteristic Finding
PR Interval Gets progressively longer
QRS Eventually dropped
Pattern Repeats

Classic Pattern

  • PR = 0.18
  • PR = 0.22
  • PR = 0.26
  • QRS drops
  • Cycle repeats
"Wenckebach gets longer, longer, longer, drop."

Location

Usually occurs at the AV node.

Second-Degree AV Block Type II (Mobitz II)

Characteristic Finding
PR Interval Constant
Dropped Beats Present
Pattern Sudden failure of conduction

Key ECG Clue

The PR interval does not progressively lengthen. QRS complexes are suddenly dropped.

Mobitz II is unstable until proven otherwise.

Why It Matters

  • High risk of progression to complete heart block
  • May produce severe bradycardia
  • May require transcutaneous pacing

Third-Degree AV Block (Complete Heart Block)

Characteristic Finding
Atrial Activity Present
Ventricular Activity Present
Relationship None
P Waves Independent
QRS Complexes Independent

AV Dissociation

The atria and ventricles are functioning independently. The atrial rhythm and ventricular rhythm have no relationship to one another.

Board Exam Pearl: More P waves than QRS complexes + no relationship = Complete Heart Block.

Pacing Indications

Consider Transcutaneous Pacing When:

  • Symptomatic bradycardia persists
  • Mobitz II produces instability
  • Third-degree AV block causes poor perfusion
  • Atropine is ineffective or unlikely to work
Mobitz II and Complete Heart Block should trigger early consideration of pacing.

Clinical Scenario #1

  • HR 58
  • PR = 0.28 sec
  • Every P wave followed by a QRS
  • BP 132/84
What rhythm is present? First-Degree AV Block
Treatment needed? Generally no. Monitor and evaluate underlying causes.

Clinical Scenario #2

  • PR intervals progressively lengthen
  • Every fourth QRS drops
  • BP 118/70
What rhythm is present? Mobitz I (Wenckebach)

Clinical Scenario #3

  • HR 36
  • Fixed PR intervals
  • Intermittent dropped QRS complexes
  • BP 80/48
  • Dizziness present
What rhythm is present? Mobitz II
Treatment Priority? Management of unstable bradycardia and preparation for pacing.

Clinical Scenario #4

  • HR 28
  • Independent P waves
  • Independent QRS complexes
  • Altered mental status
  • Cool pale skin
What rhythm is present? Third-Degree AV Block
Stable or Unstable? Unstable
Priority Intervention? Immediate treatment of symptomatic bradycardia with pacing preparation.

Rhythm Recognition Practice

PR interval 0.24 sec, every P conducted. Rhythm? First-Degree AV Block
PR gets progressively longer, then a QRS drops. Rhythm? Mobitz I
PR constant, QRS suddenly drops. Rhythm? Mobitz II
P waves and QRS complexes have no relationship. Rhythm? Third-Degree AV Block

Interactive Flashcards

Question: What is the hallmark of First-Degree AV Block?

Answer: PR interval greater than 0.20 seconds.
Question: What rhythm is summarized as "longer, longer, longer, drop"?

Answer: Mobitz I
Question: Which AV block commonly progresses to complete heart block?

Answer: Mobitz II
Question: What is AV dissociation?

Answer: Independent atrial and ventricular activity with no relationship.

NREMT-Style Questions

1. A patient has a PR interval of 0.24 sec and every atrial impulse is conducted. What AV block is present?

Answer First-Degree AV Block

2. Which AV block demonstrates progressive PR prolongation before a dropped QRS?

Answer Mobitz I

3. Which AV block has the greatest risk of progressing to complete heart block?

Answer Mobitz II

4. What ECG finding defines Third-Degree AV Block?

Answer Complete AV dissociation

5. Which advanced intervention should be strongly considered in unstable Mobitz II or Third-Degree AV Block?

Answer Transcutaneous pacing
