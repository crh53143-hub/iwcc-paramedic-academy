What Does an ECG Measure?

An electrocardiogram (ECG) records the heart's electrical activity.

The ECG does not show mechanical contraction. It records the electrical events that trigger myocardial contraction.

Remember: Electrical activity does not always equal effective perfusion. Always assess the patient, not just the monitor.

Numbers to Memorize

  • Small Box = 0.04 sec
  • Large Box = 0.20 sec
  • 5 Large Boxes = 1 second
  • 300 Large Boxes = 1 minute

Components of the ECG

Component Represents
P Wave Atrial Depolarization
PR Interval Atrial-to-Ventricular Conduction Time
QRS Complex Ventricular Depolarization
ST Segment Early Ventricular Repolarization
T Wave Ventricular Repolarization

Normal Measurements

Measurement Normal Value
PR Interval 0.12 - 0.20 sec
QRS Duration ≤ 0.12 sec
Normal Sinus Rate 60 - 100 bpm

Heart Rate Calculation

300 Method

Large Boxes Rate
1 300
2 150
3 100
4 75
5 60
6 50

Heart Rate = 300 ÷ Number of Large Boxes

Four large boxes between R waves? 75 bpm
Three large boxes between R waves? 100 bpm

Rhythm Interpretation Method

  1. Determine Heart Rate
  2. Determine Rhythm Regularity
  3. Evaluate P Waves
  4. Measure PR Interval
  5. Measure QRS Duration
  6. Determine Rhythm Origin
  7. Assess Patient Stability
Use the same method on every rhythm strip. Consistency improves accuracy.

Questions to Ask Every Time

  • Is the rhythm regular?
  • Is there a P wave before every QRS?
  • Does every P wave produce a QRS?
  • Is the PR interval normal?
  • Is the QRS narrow or wide?
  • What is the rate?
  • Is the patient stable?

NREMT-Style Questions

1. What ECG component represents ventricular depolarization?

Answer QRS Complex

2. What is the normal PR interval?

Answer 0.12 - 0.20 seconds

3. What is the normal adult sinus rate?

Answer 60 - 100 bpm

4. Four large boxes between R waves equals what rate?

Answer 75 bpm

5. Why should rhythm interpretation always be correlated with patient assessment?

Answer Electrical activity does not always produce effective mechanical activity or adequate perfusion.
