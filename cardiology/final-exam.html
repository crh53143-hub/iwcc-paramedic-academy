Section 1: Cardiac Anatomy & Physiology

1. Which cardiac chamber generates the highest pressure?

Answer Left Ventricle

2. Which vessel carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?

Answer Pulmonary Veins

3. Which coronary artery most commonly supplies the anterior wall?

Answer Left Anterior Descending (LAD)

4. What is the intrinsic rate of the AV Junction?

Answer 40–60 bpm

5. What property allows the SA node to spontaneously fire?

Answer Automaticity

Section 2: ECG Fundamentals

6. What does the P wave represent?

Answer Atrial Depolarization

7. What is the normal PR interval?

Answer 0.12–0.20 seconds

8. What does the QRS complex represent?

Answer Ventricular Depolarization

9. What does the T wave represent?

Answer Ventricular Repolarization

10. Four large boxes between R waves equals what rate?

Answer 75 bpm

Section 3: Rhythm Recognition

11. Rate 80 bpm, regular, P before every QRS.

Answer Normal Sinus Rhythm

12. Rate 45 bpm, regular, P before every QRS.

Answer Sinus Bradycardia

13. Rate 132 bpm, regular, P before every QRS.

Answer Sinus Tachycardia

14. Irregularly irregular rhythm with no discernible P waves.

Answer Atrial Fibrillation

15. Sawtooth flutter waves.

Answer Atrial Flutter

16. Three different P-wave morphologies and rate 130.

Answer Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia

17. Rate 52, absent P waves, narrow QRS.

Answer Junctional Escape Rhythm

Section 4: AV Blocks

18. PR interval greater than 0.20 with all beats conducted.

Answer First-Degree AV Block

19. Longer, longer, longer, drop.

Answer Mobitz I (Wenckebach)

20. Constant PR interval with dropped QRS complexes.

Answer Mobitz II

21. Complete AV dissociation.

Answer Third-Degree AV Block

Section 5: Ventricular Rhythms

22. Wide premature beat originating in the ventricle.

Answer PVC

23. Escape rhythm with rate 20–40 bpm.

Answer Idioventricular Rhythm

24. Wide-complex tachycardia should be treated as what until proven otherwise?

Answer Ventricular Tachycardia

25. Twisting rhythm associated with prolonged QT.

Answer Torsades de Pointes

26. Chaotic rhythm with no organized complexes.

Answer Ventricular Fibrillation

27. Organized electrical activity without a pulse.

Answer PEA

Section 6: 12 Lead ECG

28. Inferior leads?

Answer II, III, aVF

29. Anterior leads?

Answer V3, V4

30. Septal leads?

Answer V1, V2

31. Which artery commonly causes anterior STEMI?

Answer LAD

32. Inferior STEMI commonly affects which artery?

Answer RCA

33. Reciprocal changes for Inferior STEMI?

Answer Lead I and aVL depression

Section 7: Acute Coronary Syndromes

34. Which ACS condition involves ST elevation?

Answer STEMI

35. Which ACS condition has elevated troponin without ST elevation?

Answer NSTEMI

36. First-line antiplatelet medication in ACS?

Answer Aspirin

37. Most important prehospital STEMI diagnostic tool?

Answer 12-Lead ECG

38. Shock associated with pump failure?

Answer Cardiogenic Shock

Section 8: Clinical Scenarios

39. Patient presents with chest pressure, diaphoresis, ST elevation in II, III, and aVF.

Answer Inferior STEMI

40. Patient has a rate of 32 bpm with independent P waves and QRS complexes.

Answer Third-Degree AV Block

41. Patient is pulseless with ventricular tachycardia on the monitor.

Answer Pulseless VT (Shockable Rhythm)

42. Patient has organized electrical activity but remains pulseless.

Answer PEA

43. Wide-complex tachycardia at 180 bpm with hypotension and altered mental status.

Answer Unstable Ventricular Tachycardia
