Key Concept: Ventricular Rhythms

Ventricular rhythms originate below the AV junction. Because ventricular depolarization does not follow the normal conduction pathway, the QRS complex is typically wide (>0.12 sec).

High-Yield Exam Rule: Wide QRS + Ventricular Origin = Potentially Dangerous Until Proven Otherwise.

Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs)

Characteristic Finding
Origin Ectopic Ventricular Focus
P Waves Absent Before PVC
QRS Wide & Bizarre
Timing Occurs Early

Common Causes

  • Hypoxia
  • Ischemia
  • Stimulants
  • Electrolyte disturbances
  • Myocardial infarction

PVC Patterns

Pattern Description
Bigeminy Every Other Beat is a PVC
Trigeminy Every Third Beat is a PVC
Couplet Two Consecutive PVCs
Triplet Three Consecutive PVCs
Multifocal Different PVC Morphologies

Idioventricular Rhythm (IVR)

Characteristic Finding
Rate 20-40 bpm
Rhythm Regular
P Waves Absent
QRS Wide

Occurs When

  • Ventricles become the pacemaker
  • SA and AV pacemakers fail
  • Significant conduction system disease exists

Accelerated Idioventricular Rhythm (AIVR)

Characteristic Finding
Rate 41-100 bpm
P Waves Usually absent
QRS Wide

Important Association

AIVR is commonly seen following successful reperfusion of a STEMI patient.

Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)

Characteristic Finding
Rate >100 bpm
Rhythm Usually Regular
P Waves Absent or Dissociated
QRS Wide

Monomorphic VT

All ventricular complexes appear similar.

Polymorphic VT

QRS shape continuously changes.

Treat any wide-complex tachycardia as VT until proven otherwise.

Torsades de Pointes

A specific type of polymorphic ventricular tachycardia associated with prolonged QT intervals.

Characteristic Finding
Rhythm Polymorphic VT
Appearance Twisting Around Baseline
Risk May Degenerate Into VF

Common Causes

  • Prolonged QT interval
  • Hypomagnesemia
  • Certain antiarrhythmics
  • Congenital long QT syndrome
ACLS Pearl: Magnesium sulfate is the medication most often associated with Torsades treatment.

Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)

Characteristic Finding
Electrical Activity Chaotic
Cardiac Output None
Pulse Absent
Priority Immediate Defibrillation
VF is a shockable rhythm.

Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA)

PEA occurs when organized electrical activity exists but no effective pulse is present.

Common Reversible Causes

  • Hypovolemia
  • Hypoxia
  • Hydrogen Ion Excess
  • Hypo/Hyperkalemia
  • Hypothermia
  • Tension Pneumothorax
  • Cardiac Tamponade
  • Toxins
  • Pulmonary Thrombosis
  • Coronary Thrombosis
PEA is NOT shockable.

Asystole

Characteristic Finding
Electrical Activity Absent
Cardiac Output None
Pulse Absent
Asystole is NOT shockable.

Shockable vs Non-Shockable Rhythms

Shockable Non-Shockable
Ventricular Fibrillation PEA
Pulseless VT Asystole

Clinical Scenarios

Patient #1

  • Rate 180
  • Wide-complex tachycardia
  • BP 68/38
  • Altered mental status
Interpretation Unstable Ventricular Tachycardia

Patient #2

  • No pulse
  • Chaotic waveform
Interpretation Ventricular Fibrillation

Patient #3

  • Organized electrical activity
  • No palpable pulse
Interpretation PEA

Rhythm Recognition Practice

Rate 32 bpm with wide QRS complexes. Rhythm? Idioventricular Rhythm
Rate 76 bpm with ventricular origin and wide complexes. Rhythm? Accelerated Idioventricular Rhythm
Wide-complex tachycardia at 170 bpm. Rhythm? Ventricular Tachycardia
Twisting polymorphic ventricular rhythm. Rhythm? Torsades de Pointes
Chaotic rhythm without organized complexes. Rhythm? Ventricular Fibrillation

NREMT/ACLS Review Questions

1. What rhythm should be presumed when a patient has a wide-complex tachycardia?

Answer Ventricular Tachycardia until proven otherwise.

2. What medication is commonly associated with treatment of Torsades?

Answer Magnesium Sulfate

3. Which ventricular rhythm commonly occurs after successful reperfusion therapy?

Answer Accelerated Idioventricular Rhythm

4. Which rhythms are shockable?

Answer VF and Pulseless VT

5. Which rhythms are NOT shockable?

Answer PEA and Asystole
