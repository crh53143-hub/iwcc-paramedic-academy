What is Acute Coronary Syndrome?

Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) is a spectrum of conditions caused by reduced coronary artery blood flow resulting in myocardial ischemia or infarction.

ACS Includes

  • Unstable Angina
  • NSTEMI
  • STEMI
Think of ACS as a continuum ranging from ischemia to complete myocardial infarction.

Pathophysiology

Step 1

Atherosclerotic plaque develops in a coronary artery.

Step 2

Plaque rupture occurs.

Step 3

Platelet aggregation and thrombus formation begin.

Step 4

Coronary blood flow decreases.

Step 5

Myocardial ischemia, injury, and infarction occur.

Ischemia vs Injury vs Infarction

Condition Meaning Typical ECG Finding
Ischemia Reduced oxygen supply T-wave inversion
Injury Current myocardial damage ST elevation
Infarction Cell death Pathologic Q waves

Stable Angina

Feature Description
Trigger Exertion or stress
Relief Rest or Nitroglycerin
Troponins Negative
Permanent Damage No

Unstable Angina

Feature Description
Occurs at Rest Yes
Predictability Unpredictable
Troponins Negative
Risk High Risk ACS
Unstable Angina is a medical emergency despite a lack of myocardial necrosis.

NSTEMI

Finding Typical Presentation
Troponin Elevated
ST Elevation Absent
Tissue Damage Present

Common ECG Findings

  • ST Depression
  • T-wave inversion
  • Nonspecific changes

STEMI

Finding Typical Presentation
Troponin Elevated
ST Elevation Present
Coronary Occlusion Usually Complete
Time is myocardium. STEMI recognition and rapid reperfusion are critical.

Classic ACS Presentation

  • Chest pressure
  • Chest heaviness
  • Crushing discomfort
  • Substernal pain
  • Diaphoresis
  • Nausea
  • Shortness of breath
  • Pain radiating to jaw, arm, neck, or back

Atypical Presentations

Common in:

  • Older adults
  • Women
  • Diabetic patients
  • Patients with heart failure

Possible Complaints

  • Weakness
  • Syncope
  • Fatigue
  • Dyspnea
  • Indigestion
  • Nausea

ACS Assessment Priorities

  1. Airway
  2. Breathing
  3. Circulation
  4. Vital Signs
  5. 12-Lead ECG
  6. Focused History
  7. Pain Assessment
  8. Risk Factor Evaluation

Risk Factors

  • Hypertension
  • Diabetes
  • Hyperlipidemia
  • Smoking
  • Obesity
  • Family history
  • Sedentary lifestyle
  • Known coronary artery disease

Prehospital Treatment Priorities

Intervention Purpose
12-Lead ECG Identify ischemia or infarction
Aspirin Reduce platelet aggregation
Nitroglycerin* Reduce workload and improve perfusion
IV Access Medication administration
Cardiac Monitoring Detect dysrhythmias

*Follow local protocol and assess contraindications.

Nitroglycerin Cautions

  • Hypotension
  • Recent PDE-5 inhibitor use
  • Suspected right ventricular infarction
Inferior STEMI + Hypotension = Consider Right Ventricular MI before administering nitroglycerin.

Cardiogenic Shock

Cardiogenic shock occurs when myocardial pump failure prevents adequate tissue perfusion.

Common Findings

  • Hypotension
  • Cool clammy skin
  • Altered mental status
  • Pulmonary edema
  • Tachycardia

Clinical Scenario #1

  • Chest pressure for 45 minutes
  • ST elevation in II, III, and aVF
  • BP 132/80
  • Diaphoretic
Diagnosis? Inferior STEMI
Treatment Priorities? Rapid STEMI recognition, aspirin, 12-lead transmission if available, cardiac monitoring, transport to PCI-capable facility.

Clinical Scenario #2

  • Chest discomfort at rest
  • No ST elevation
  • Known CAD
  • Symptoms worsening over several days
Most Concerning Diagnosis? Unstable Angina

Clinical Scenario #3

  • Chest pain
  • Shortness of breath
  • BP 82/48
  • Cool, pale skin
  • Crackles present
Most Concerning Complication? Cardiogenic Shock

Interactive Flashcards

Question: What does ST elevation represent?

Answer: Myocardial injury
Question: What biomarker is elevated in NSTEMI and STEMI?

Answer: Troponin
Question: Which ACS condition has elevated troponin but no ST elevation?

Answer: NSTEMI
Question: What is the first medication typically given for ACS?

Answer: Aspirin (per local protocol)

NREMT Review Questions

1. Which ACS condition generally involves complete coronary occlusion?

Answer STEMI

2. Which ACS condition has myocardial necrosis without ST elevation?

Answer NSTEMI

3. Which ECG change is most associated with myocardial injury?

Answer ST Elevation

4. What is the most important prehospital diagnostic tool for STEMI recognition?

Answer 12-Lead ECG

5. What shock state commonly develops after extensive myocardial damage?

Answer Cardiogenic Shock
