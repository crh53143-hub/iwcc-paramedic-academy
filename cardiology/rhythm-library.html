Sinus Rhythm Recognition

Normal Sinus Rhythm

  • 60-100 bpm
  • Regular
  • P before every QRS
  • PR 0.12-0.20
  • Narrow QRS

Sinus Bradycardia

  • Rate <60 bpm
  • Regular rhythm
  • Normal P waves
  • Usually narrow QRS

Sinus Tachycardia

  • Rate >100 bpm
  • Regular rhythm
  • Normal P waves
  • Narrow QRS

Atrial Rhythm Recognition

PACs

  • Premature beat
  • Abnormal P wave
  • Underlying rhythm interrupted

Atrial Flutter

  • Sawtooth waves
  • Atrial rate 250-350
  • Often ventricular rate near 150

Atrial Fibrillation

  • Irregularly irregular
  • No identifiable P waves
  • Variable ventricular response

Junctional Rhythm Recognition

Junctional Escape Rhythm

  • 40-60 bpm
  • Absent or inverted P waves
  • Narrow QRS

Accelerated Junctional Rhythm

  • 61-100 bpm
  • Retrograde P waves
  • Narrow QRS

Junctional Tachycardia

  • >100 bpm
  • Regular rhythm
  • Digoxin toxicity association

AV Block Recognition

First Degree AV Block

  • PR >0.20 sec
  • Every P conducted

Mobitz I

  • Longer, Longer, Longer, Drop
  • Progressive PR prolongation

Mobitz II

  • Constant PR Interval
  • Sudden dropped QRS
  • Dangerous rhythm

Third Degree AV Block

  • Complete AV Dissociation
  • No relationship between P waves and QRS complexes

Ventricular Rhythm Recognition

PVC

  • Early beat
  • Wide bizarre QRS

Idioventricular Rhythm

  • 20-40 bpm
  • Wide QRS

Accelerated Idioventricular Rhythm

  • 41-100 bpm
  • Reperfusion rhythm

Ventricular Tachycardia

  • Wide-complex tachycardia
  • Treat as VT until proven otherwise

Torsades de Pointes

  • Twisting morphology
  • Associated with prolonged QT

Ventricular Fibrillation

  • Chaotic waveform
  • No cardiac output
  • Shockable

Asystole

  • No electrical activity
  • Not shockable

30 Second Rhythm Challenge

Scenario 1

Rate 44 bpm, regular, P before every QRS.

Answer Sinus Bradycardia

Scenario 2

Rate 150 bpm, sawtooth pattern.

Answer Atrial Flutter

Scenario 3

Irregularly irregular, no visible P waves.

Answer Atrial Fibrillation

Scenario 4

Regular wide-complex tachycardia at 180 bpm.

Answer Ventricular Tachycardia

Scenario 5

Progressively longer PR intervals followed by a dropped QRS.

Answer Mobitz I
