Why 12 Leads Matter

A rhythm strip tells you how the electrical system is functioning. A 12-lead ECG tells you how the myocardium is functioning.

Registry Pearl: A patient may have a normal rhythm but still have an acute STEMI.

The 12 Leads

Lead Group Leads View
Inferior II, III, aVF Bottom of heart
Septal V1, V2 Septum
Anterior V3, V4 Front wall

Coronary Artery Correlation

MI Location Common Vessel
Inferior Right Coronary Artery (RCA)
Anterior Left Anterior Descending (LAD)
Septal LAD
Lateral Circumflex
Inferior STEMI + Bradycardia = Think RCA involvement.

STEMI Criteria

STEMI recognition requires ST elevation in two or more contiguous leads.

Contiguous Lead Groups

  • II, III, aVF = Inferior
  • V1, V2 = Septal
  • V3, V4 = Anterior
  • I, aVL, V5, V6 = Lateral

Systematic 12-Lead Interpretation

  1. Assess Heart Rate
  2. Assess Rhythm
  3. Determine QRS Width
  4. Evaluate Cardiac Axis
  5. Assess ST Segments
  6. Look for Reciprocal Changes
  7. Identify Infarct Location
  8. Correlate Findings with Patient Presentation
Use the same process on every patient. Consistency improves STEMI recognition.

Inferior STEMI

Look For:

  • ST Elevation in II, III, aVF
  • Reciprocal depression in I and aVL

Associated Findings

  • Bradycardia
  • Hypotension
  • AV Blocks

Anterior STEMI

Look For:

  • ST Elevation in V3 and V4
  • May extend into V1, V2, V5, and V6
Anterior STEMIs are frequently large infarctions and can rapidly deteriorate.

Lateral STEMI

Look For:

  • ST Elevation in I and aVL
  • ST Elevation in V5 and V6

Reciprocal Changes

Reciprocal changes occur opposite the area of infarction and strengthen the diagnosis of STEMI.

STEMI Location Possible Reciprocal Leads
Inferior I and aVL
Lateral II, III, aVF

Clinical Scenario #1

  • Chest pressure
  • Diaphoresis
  • ST elevation in II, III, aVF
  • Reciprocal depression in aVL
Interpretation Inferior STEMI
Likely Artery? Right Coronary Artery

Clinical Scenario #2

  • Chest pain
  • V1-V4 ST Elevation
  • Tachycardia
Interpretation Anterior STEMI
Likely Artery? LAD

STEMI Localization Practice

ST elevation in II, III, aVF Inferior STEMI
ST elevation in V1-V2 Septal STEMI
ST elevation in V3-V4 Anterior STEMI
ST elevation in I, aVL, V5, V6 Lateral STEMI

NREMT & STEMI Review Questions

1. Which leads view the inferior wall?

Answer II, III, aVF

2. Which artery is most commonly associated with anterior STEMI?

Answer LAD

3. What reciprocal changes are commonly seen with an inferior STEMI?

Answer ST depression in I and aVL

4. Which MI location is most commonly associated with bradycardia?

Answer Inferior STEMI

5. What should be assessed after identifying ST elevation?

Answer Reciprocal changes and clinical presentation
