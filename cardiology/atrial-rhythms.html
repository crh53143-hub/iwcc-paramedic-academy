Atrial Rhythm Overview

Atrial rhythms originate from ectopic sites within the atria rather than the SA node. These rhythms often produce abnormal P-wave morphology and may increase the risk of decreased cardiac output, especially at rapid rates.

When evaluating atrial rhythms ask:
  • Can I identify a P wave?
  • Do all P waves look the same?
  • Is the rhythm regular or irregular?
  • What is the ventricular rate?
  • Is the patient stable?

Premature Atrial Contractions (PACs)

Characteristic Finding
Origin Ectopic atrial focus
Rate Usually normal underlying rhythm
Rhythm Irregular due to premature beat
P Wave Premature and abnormal-looking
QRS Usually narrow

Common Causes

  • Stress
  • Caffeine
  • Tobacco
  • Hypoxia
  • Electrolyte imbalances
  • Cardiac ischemia

Wandering Atrial Pacemaker (WAP)

Characteristic Finding
Rate <100 bpm
Rhythm Irregular
P Waves At least 3 different morphologies
QRS Narrow
Key ECG Clue: Three or more different P-wave shapes.

Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT)

Characteristic Finding
Rate >100 bpm
Rhythm Irregular
P Waves 3 or more morphologies
QRS Narrow

Common Association

  • COPD
  • Chronic lung disease
  • Hypoxia
  • Respiratory failure
WAP + Rate >100 = MAT

Atrial Tachycardia

Characteristic Finding
Rate 150-250 bpm
Rhythm Usually regular
P Waves Abnormal morphology
QRS Narrow

Clinical Significance

Rapid atrial rates shorten ventricular filling time and may decrease cardiac output.

Atrial Flutter

Characteristic Finding
Atrial Rate 250-350 bpm
Rhythm Often regular
P Waves Absent
Flutter Waves Sawtooth appearance

Common Conduction Ratios

  • 2:1 Block = Ventricular rate near 150
  • 3:1 Block = Ventricular rate near 100
  • 4:1 Block = Ventricular rate near 75
A ventricular rate near 150 should always make you consider atrial flutter with 2:1 conduction.

Atrial Fibrillation

Characteristic Finding
Atrial Activity Chaotic
Rhythm Irregularly irregular
P Waves Absent
Baseline Fibrillatory waves
QRS Usually narrow

Clinical Concerns

  • Stroke risk
  • Atrial thrombus formation
  • Rapid ventricular response
  • Reduced cardiac output
Board Examination Pearl: Irregularly irregular without discernible P waves = Atrial Fibrillation

Rhythm Recognition Practice

Three different P-wave morphologies with a rate of 78 bpm. Rhythm? Wandering Atrial Pacemaker
Three different P-wave morphologies with a rate of 128 bpm. Rhythm? Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia
Irregularly irregular rhythm with no identifiable P waves. Rhythm? Atrial Fibrillation
Sawtooth baseline with ventricular rate near 150. Rhythm? Atrial Flutter with 2:1 conduction

Clinical Decision Scenarios

Patient #1

  • Rate: 160
  • BP: 118/74
  • No chest pain
  • Rhythm: Regular narrow complex tachycardia
Stable or Unstable? Stable

Patient #2

  • Rate: 170
  • BP: 72/40
  • Altered Mental Status
  • Diaphoretic
Stable or Unstable? Unstable
Treatment Priority? Immediate synchronized cardioversion if rhythm and clinical context support unstable tachycardia.

Interactive Flashcards

Question: What rhythm is defined as irregularly irregular?

Answer: Atrial Fibrillation
Question: What rhythm produces sawtooth flutter waves?

Answer: Atrial Flutter
Question: What distinguishes WAP from MAT?

Answer: Heart Rate (MAT >100 bpm)
Question: How many P-wave morphologies are needed for MAT?

Answer: Three or more

NREMT-Style Questions

1. Which atrial rhythm has no discernible P waves and is irregularly irregular?

Answer Atrial Fibrillation

2. A COPD patient presents with an irregular rhythm, rate of 130, and three different P-wave morphologies. What rhythm is most likely?

Answer Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia

3. What ECG finding is most characteristic of atrial flutter?

Answer Sawtooth flutter waves

4. Which atrial dysrhythmia carries the highest risk of thromboembolic stroke?

Answer Atrial Fibrillation

5. What is the major difference between WAP and MAT?

Answer Heart rate. MAT exceeds 100 bpm.
