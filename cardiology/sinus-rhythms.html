What Makes a Rhythm Sinus?

A rhythm is considered sinus in origin when the electrical impulse begins in the sinoatrial (SA) node and follows the normal cardiac conduction pathway.

Sinus Rhythm Criteria

  • P wave before every QRS
  • Every P wave produces a QRS complex
  • P waves look similar throughout the rhythm strip
  • PR interval remains consistent
  • QRS complexes are narrow (≤ 0.12 sec)
Remember: The rate may change, but if the impulse comes from the SA node, it remains a sinus rhythm.

Normal Sinus Rhythm (NSR)

Characteristic Normal Value
Rate 60–100 bpm
Rhythm Regular
P Waves Present and uniform
PR Interval 0.12–0.20 sec
QRS ≤ 0.12 sec

Rhythm Recognition Checklist

  • Rate 60-100?
  • P wave before every QRS?
  • Regular rhythm?
  • PR interval normal?
  • Narrow QRS?

Sinus Bradycardia

Sinus Bradycardia is a sinus rhythm with a heart rate less than 60 bpm.

Characteristic Finding
Rate < 60 bpm
Rhythm Regular
P Waves Present and uniform
PR Interval Normal
QRS Normal

Common Causes

  • Sleep
  • Well-conditioned athletes
  • Inferior MI
  • Beta-blockers
  • Calcium channel blockers
  • Increased vagal tone
  • Hypothermia

Signs & Symptoms

  • Dizziness
  • Weakness
  • Hypotension
  • Syncope
  • Altered mental status
Treat the patient, not the monitor. Many patients tolerate rates in the 40s without symptoms.

Sinus Tachycardia

Sinus Tachycardia is a sinus rhythm with a heart rate greater than 100 bpm.

Characteristic Finding
Rate 100–160+ bpm
Rhythm Regular
P Waves Present and uniform
PR Interval Normal
QRS Normal

Common Causes

  • Pain
  • Fear
  • Anxiety
  • Hypovolemia
  • Shock
  • Fever
  • Hypoxia
  • Exercise
  • Heart Failure
Sinus Tachycardia is usually a symptom of another problem. Find and treat the underlying cause.

Sinus Arrhythmia

Sinus Arrhythmia occurs when the rhythm varies with the respiratory cycle.

  • Rate usually normal
  • P waves remain normal
  • PR interval remains normal
  • QRS remains normal
  • Rhythm is irregular

Typical Pattern

The heart rate increases during inspiration and decreases during expiration.

Sinus arrhythmia is common in younger healthy patients and is usually considered benign.

Clinical Decision Making

Patient #1

  • HR 48
  • BP 126/74
  • No complaints
  • Physically active runner
Stable or Unstable? Stable. No treatment indicated.

Patient #2

  • HR 38
  • BP 72/40
  • Altered Mental Status
  • Cool skin
Stable or Unstable? Unstable symptomatic bradycardia.
Treatment Priority? Atropine, pacing preparation, airway support, and assessment of underlying causes.

NREMT-Style Questions

1. A patient has a rate of 44 bpm, a P wave before every QRS, and a regular rhythm. What rhythm is present?

Answer Sinus Bradycardia

2. Which assessment finding is most concerning in a patient with bradycardia?

Answer Hypotension with signs of poor perfusion

3. A patient has a rate of 132 bpm after severe blood loss. What is the most likely rhythm?

Answer Sinus Tachycardia

4. Sinus Arrhythmia is most commonly associated with:

Answer Respiratory variation

5. What determines whether a bradycardic patient requires treatment?

Answer Patient symptoms and evidence of poor perfusion
