Review: The AV Junction

The AV junction includes the AV node and adjacent tissue. Normally, the SA node controls the heart rate. If the SA node slows significantly or fails, the AV junction can assume pacemaker responsibility.

Intrinsic Pacemaker Rates

Pacemaker Site Intrinsic Rate
SA Node 60–100 bpm
AV Junction 40–60 bpm
Ventricles 20–40 bpm
Think "escape rhythm." The AV junction often takes over because the SA node is too slow or impulses fail to reach the ventricles.

Recognizing Junctional Rhythms

Typical ECG Features

  • Regular rhythm
  • Narrow QRS complex
  • P waves absent, inverted, or occurring after the QRS
  • Originates from the AV junction

Types of P Waves

  1. Inverted before the QRS
  2. Buried within the QRS
  3. Inverted after the QRS
Exam Pearl: Any of these three P-wave presentations may occur in junctional rhythms.

Junctional Escape Rhythm

Characteristic Finding
Rate 40–60 bpm
Rhythm Regular
P Wave Absent, inverted, or after QRS
QRS Narrow

Common Causes

  • SA node failure
  • Inferior MI
  • Increased vagal tone
  • Digitalis effects
  • Sick sinus syndrome

Accelerated Junctional Rhythm

Characteristic Finding
Rate 61–100 bpm
Rhythm Regular
P Waves Absent or inverted
QRS Narrow

Clinical Importance

Occurs when the AV junction becomes more automatic than the SA node.

Rate is the key difference between a Junctional Escape Rhythm and an Accelerated Junctional Rhythm.

Junctional Tachycardia

Characteristic Finding
Rate >100 bpm
Rhythm Regular
P Waves Absent or inverted
QRS Narrow

Common Causes

  • Digoxin toxicity
  • Myocardial ischemia
  • Hypoxia
  • Cardiac surgery
  • Electrolyte disturbances
Junctional Tachycardia should always raise suspicion for Digoxin toxicity on exams.

Rate Comparison

Rhythm Rate
Junctional Escape Rhythm 40–60 bpm
Accelerated Junctional Rhythm 61–100 bpm
Junctional Tachycardia >100 bpm

Clinical Decision Making

Patient #1

  • HR 46
  • BP 124/72
  • No symptoms
  • Regular narrow-complex rhythm
Most likely rhythm? Junctional Escape Rhythm
Treatment needed? Likely none. Monitor and evaluate underlying causes.

Patient #2

  • HR 42
  • BP 74/40
  • Altered Mental Status
  • Cool, pale skin
Stable or Unstable? Unstable
Treatment Priority? Manage symptomatic bradycardia, support ABCs, prepare for pacing and medication therapy according to protocol.

Rhythm Recognition Practice

Rate 52 bpm. No visible P waves. Narrow QRS. Rhythm? Junctional Escape Rhythm
Rate 82 bpm. Inverted P waves. Narrow QRS. Rhythm? Accelerated Junctional Rhythm
Rate 124 bpm. Retrograde P waves after QRS complexes. Rhythm? Junctional Tachycardia
What rate range defines a Junctional Escape Rhythm? 40–60 bpm

Interactive Flashcards

Question: What is the intrinsic rate of the AV Junction?

Answer: 40–60 bpm
Question: What does an inverted P wave suggest?

Answer: Impulse is traveling backward through the atria (retrograde conduction).
Question: What rhythm should raise concern for Digoxin toxicity?

Answer: Junctional Tachycardia
Question: What is Accelerated Junctional Rhythm's rate range?

Answer: 61–100 bpm

NREMT-Style Questions

1. A rhythm demonstrates a rate of 48 bpm, absent P waves, and a narrow QRS. What rhythm is most likely present?

Answer Junctional Escape Rhythm

2. What is the intrinsic rate of the AV junction?

Answer 40–60 bpm

3. What rhythm has a rate of 70 bpm with junctional characteristics?

Answer Accelerated Junctional Rhythm

4. What electrolyte or medication-related toxicity is commonly associated with Junctional Tachycardia?

Answer Digoxin toxicity

5. What are the three possible P-wave locations in a junctional rhythm?

Answer Before QRS (inverted), buried within QRS, or after QRS (retrograde).
